# Master Literature / Bibliography

Cross-case index of the heavier **academic and primary sources** gathered in deep
research, grouped by domain so later cases can reuse them. Per-case detail lives in
each case's `deep-research.md` and `sources.md`.

> **Verification status:** most items were index-confirmed via search but **not
> opened full-text** (proxy blocked scholarly hosts). Treat findings as
> abstract-level paraphrases; re-verify wording and confirm flagged bibliographic
> details before quoting in the final article.

Tags: **[EV]** evidence (peer-reviewed / data / primary report) · **[OP]** opinion
(book-length argument, essay, journalism) · **[PRIMARY]** primary/reference.

## 1. WoW / MMO guilds as organizations  · used by: CASE-001, CASE-002

- **[EV]** Williams, Ducheneaut, Xiong, Zhang, Yee & Nickell (2006). *From Tree
  House to Barracks: The Social Life of Guilds in WoW.* Games and Culture 1(4):338–361.
  DOI 10.1177/1555412006292616. — casual↔"barracks" typology; mechanics moderate
  social outcomes; elitism a top dissatisfaction source.
- **[EV]** Ducheneaut, Yee, Nickell & Moore (2007). *The Life and Death of Online
  Gaming Communities.* CHI 2007. DOI 10.1145/1240624.1240750. — guilds routinely
  collapse; survival predicted by size, **class balance**, connection density; ~25% churn.
- **[EV]** Ducheneaut, Yee, Nickell & Moore (2006). *"Alone Together?"* CHI 2006.
  DOI 10.1145/1124772.1124834. — much guild sociality is thin/co-present; deep
  interdependence concentrated at raiding endgame.
- **[EV]** Nardi & Harris (2006). *Strangers and Friends: Collaborative Play in WoW.*
  CSCW 2006. DOI 10.1145/1180875.1180898. — coordination load escalates with difficulty.
- **[EV]** Nardi (2010). *My Life as a Night Elf Priest.* Univ. Michigan Press (OA).
  — play/work blur, from a healer POV.
- **[EV]** Yee (2006). *The Labor of Fun.* Games and Culture 1(1):68–71. DOI
  10.1177/1555412005281819. — MMO play as "a second job"; behavioral conditioning.
- **[EV]** Lukacs, Embrick & Wright (2010). *The Managed Hearthstone: Labor and
  Emotional Work in WoW.* Springer LNICST 33. DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-11743-5_13
  *(confirm authors)*. — Hochschild **emotional labor** applied to WoW.
- **[EV]** Poor & Skoric (2014). *Death of a Guild, Birth of a Network.* Games and
  Culture 9. DOI 10.1177/1555412014537401. — subgroup schism kills guilds; leader
  power is mechanically concentrated; network outlives the guild.
- **[EV]** Chen (2012). *Leet Noobs: The Life and Death of an Expert Player Group in
  WoW.* Peter Lang. — DKP/loot economies "legitimize hierarchies"; raid group melts down.
- **[EV]** Debeauvais, Nardi, Schiano, Ducheneaut & Yee (2011). *If You Build It They
  Might Stay: Retention Mechanisms in WoW.* FDG '11. — retention is designed; real
  friends/family = strongest retention lever. *(complicates coercion reading)*
- **[EV]** Guild-commitment via Rusbult's **Investment Model** (Computers in Human
  Behavior, 2014; *confirm authors*). DOI S074756321400065X. — satisfaction + sunk
  investment + poor alternatives → commitment. *(complicates coercion reading)*
- **[EV]** Ahlström & Fors (2024). *Play Hard, Work Harder: Workification of Gaming
  in a Swedish WoW **Classic** Guild.* ephemera. — era-matched "**workification**."
- **[EV]** Malone (~2007). *Governance and Economy in a Virtual World: Guild
  Organization in WoW.* (UW–Milwaukee; *confirm type*). — guild as polity/firm.
- **[EV]** Snodgrass et al. (2017). *A guild culture of "casual raiding" enhances its
  members' online gaming experiences.* New Media & Society. DOI 10.1177/1461444816644804.
  *(counter-case: anti-elitist guilds do better)*
- **[EV]** Steinkuehler & Williams (2006). *Where Everybody Knows Your (Screen) Name:
  Online Games as "Third Places."* *(positive-sum view of guild sociality)*

## 2. Status, "gaming capital," metric-as-norm in games · used by: CASE-002

- **[EV]** T.L. Taylor (2006). *Play Between Worlds*, ch. "Beyond Fun: Instrumental
  Play and Power Gamers." MIT Press (OA). — power-gamer norms become mandatory.
- **[EV]** Consalvo (2007). *Cheating: Gaining Advantage in Videogames.* MIT Press. —
  **"gaming capital"** (score/knowledge as social currency ≈ a credential).
- **[EV]** Ask (2016). *The Value of Calculations…* Bulletin of Science, Technology &
  Society. DOI 10.1177/0270467617690058. — theorycraft *stabilizes/enforces* one style.
- **[EV]** Yee, Daedalus Project — Achievement (Advancement/Mechanics/Competition)
  motivation cluster; ~40k respondents.
- **[EV]** Steinkuehler & Duncan (2008). *Scientific Habits of Mind in Virtual
  Worlds.* J. Sci. Educ. Tech. 17(6). DOI 10.1007/s10956-008-9120-8. *(complicates
  "score ≠ skill": real competence circulates → score is a biased proxy, not empty)*
- **[PRIMARY]** Wowpedia, *Gear score*; Raider.IO interviews (TeamLiquid; Wowhead
  #380545); GearScore dev interview (Stormstrike 2009).

## 3. Loyalty, "family" rhetoric, motivation · used by: CASE-001

- **[EV]** Stanley, Neck & Neck (2023). *Loyal workers are selectively and ironically
  targeted for exploitation.* J. Experimental Social Psychology 106:104442. DOI
  10.1016/j.jesp.2022.104442. — loyalty → more unpaid work; self-reinforcing.
- **[EV]** Kunda (1992/2006). *Engineering Culture.* Temple UP. — **normative control**.
- **[OP]** Rousseau (2021). *The Toxic Effects of Branding Your Workplace a "Family."*
  HBR.
- **[EV]** Bond (2025). *Cut to the Curve: Underrecognition and Talent Loss from
  Forced Ranking.* Management Science. DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2023.01204. — downgraded
  stars **≥34% more likely to quit** despite larger bonuses.
- **[EV]** Bolino, Hsiung, Harvey & LePine (2015). *Citizenship Fatigue.* J. Applied
  Psychology 100(1):56–74. DOI 10.1037/a0037583.
- **[OP/EV]** Grant (2013), *Give and Take*; Grant & Rebele (2017), *Beat Generosity
  Burnout* (HBR); Cross et al. (2016), *Collaborative Overload* (HBR).
- **[EV]** Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999). Meta-analysis, Psychological Bulletin
  125(6):627–668. DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627. — controlling rewards undermine
  intrinsic motivation.
- **[EV]** Frey & Jegen (2001). *Motivation Crowding Theory.* J. Economic Surveys
  15(5):589–611. DOI 10.1111/1467-6419.00150.
- **[EV]** Meyer & Allen (1991). *Three-component model of organizational commitment.*
  HRM Review 1(1):61–89. *(affective vs. continuance vs. normative — complicates)*

## 4. Nurses / essential-role retention (real-world "healers") · used by: CASE-001

- **[EV]** Aiken et al. (2002). *Hospital Nurse Staffing and Patient Mortality…* JAMA
  288(16):1987–1993. — +1 patient/nurse: +7% mortality, +23% burnout odds.
- **[EV]** Aiken et al. (2012). 12-country study. BMJ 344:e1717. DOI 10.1136/bmj.e1717.
  — work-environment improvement attenuates burnout.
- **[EV]** *Nurse Staffing, Work Hours, Mandatory Overtime, and Turnover* (2024). Int.
  J. Public Health 69:1607068. DOI 10.3389/ijph.2024.1607068. — **coerced overtime**
  is the factor tied to intent-to-leave; recommend banning it.

## 5. Credentialism, signaling, hiring asymmetry · used by: CASE-002

- **[EV/PRIMARY]** Fuller & Raman (2017). *Dismissed by Degrees.* HBS/Accenture/Grads
  of Life. — 67% of postings require a degree vs. 16% of incumbents.
- **[EV/PRIMARY]** Fuller et al. (2022). *The Emerging Degree Reset.* Burning Glass/HBS.
- **[EV/PRIMARY]** Burning Glass/HBS (2024). *Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road…* —
  degree drops changed **<1 in 700 hires**.
- **[OP/THEORY]** Collins (1979). *The Credential Society.* — credential inflation as
  occupational closure.
- **[EV]** Spence (1973). *Job Market Signaling.* QJE 87(3):355–374. — education as signal.
- **[OP]** Caplan (2018). *The Case Against Education.* *(80% signaling — contested)*
- **[OP/THEORY]** Labaree (1997). *How to Succeed in School Without Really Learning.* —
  credentials as positional goods → escalation.
- **[EV]** Kim & Low (2026). *Who Hires Whom? Connected Hiring in the CEO Labor
  Market.* J. Empirical Finance 87 *(confirm authors)*. — 37% of external CEOs
  connected to hiring board (vs ~5% chance); +35% appointment odds.
- **[EV]** McPherson, Smith-Lovin & Cook (2001). *Birds of a Feather: Homophily.*
  Annual Review of Sociology 27:415–444.
- **[EV]** Rivera (2015). *Pedigree: How Elite Students Get Elite Jobs.* Princeton UP.
  — cultural matching / "fit."
- **[EV]** Baert (2018). *Hiring a Gay Man, Taking a Risk?* J. Homosexuality 65(8)
  *(confirm authorship)*. — risk aversion → conformist hiring.
- **[EV]** interviewing.io. *Do LeetCode ratings predict interview performance?* —
  weak correlation (~0.17). *(verify coefficients live)*
- **[EV]** Schmidt & Hunter (1998). *Validity and Utility of Selection Methods.*
  Psychological Bulletin 124(2):262–274. *(structured interviews r≈.51 — complicates)*
- **[OP]** Larson, *Interviewing engineering executives* (lethain.com).
