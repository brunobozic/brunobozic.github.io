## The single best payoff: concertive control

**Barker (1993), "Tightening the Iron Cage," *ASQ*** is arguably the tightest academic
match in the entire project. It is the empirical demonstration that **outsourcing
control to the peer group intensifies enforcement while removing management as the
visible enforcer** — i.e., "the pressure came mostly from players, not leadership,"
proven in a factory. Tompkins & Cheney supply the term (control with *no visible
controller* = built-in deniability); Sewell names the vertical-data + horizontal-peer
surveillance combination (the corporate twin of damage-meter/surveillance addons).

## Why this case matters to the argument

- **It's the sequel to CASE-004.** 004 traps the worker in the role; 005 **welds the
  exit shut** (exit pre-stigmatized, peer-policed, reputationally punished). Together
  they enclose the burdened worker.
- **It's powered by CASE-003's engine.** Installing proxies and diffusing
  responsibility *is* self-protection under weak accountability; **moral disengagement
  (Bandura) is the same self-justification family as CASE-003's self-serving
  attribution** — here enabling harm rather than excusing failure.
- **It turns CASE-001's "family/loyalty" rhetoric against leavers** — loyalty as
  one-way, devotion-as-coercion (Blair-Loy, Reid).
- **Best concrete image for a general reader:** the "shit umbrella" inverted into a
  **deniable funnel** (Sutton) — leaders choose whether to shield or to funnel coercion
  downward with their name filed off.

## CONTRADICTS / COMPLICATES (the boundary — see `synthesis.md`)

- **Peer/clan control is often genuinely healthy** — Barker's own subjects initially
  chose and valued it; the pathology is **intensification + inescapability +
  orchestration-with-deniability**, not team-based control as such.
- **Some reputation-sharing is legitimate** (truthful, proportionate references) — the
  line is truthfulness + proportionality + retaliatory intent. (WoW twin: blacklists
  began as thief-defense; the wrong is the category error of aiming them at leavers.)
- **Exit stigma is sometimes organic; fear of exit isn't pure paranoia** — turnover is
  contagious (Felps et al. 2009), so peer influence runs both ways. The thesis is
  strongest where organic dynamics are **deliberately amplified and steered** by
  leadership that stays off the record — *orchestration + deniability* is the tell.
- The guild-leadership **advice literature itself condemns** shaming quitters — a
  recognized abuse, not a community norm.
