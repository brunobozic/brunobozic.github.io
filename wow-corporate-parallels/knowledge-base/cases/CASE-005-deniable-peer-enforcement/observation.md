# CASE-005 — Deniable Enforcement: Peer Policing, Exit Vilification & Blacklisting · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted (research pass in progress) · **Rating (from synthesis):** TBD

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> And then there was the **vilification of people who quit / rerolled** as the worst
> kind of person — someone who didn't belong in the guild in the first place, because
> "we only need dedicated, loyal team players," and the others need to be driven out
> and burnt on a stake. It was a campaign directed at getting **other raiders to exert
> the pressure and police the healers**, so that at some point the pressure came mostly
> from *players*, not from guild leadership — because leadership seemingly learned they
> should not allow themselves to be directly involved or responsible when someone
> critical rerolls or quits. Instead they **installed proxies for the rage** when that
> happens, securing **plausible deniability** and a "shit umbrella."
>
> Also, when healers left and the raid group / guild disbanded, the **healers were
> blamed** for it — their role in bringing the guild down was **advertised in public
> channels**, so they had to change character names or be blacklisted on the server.

**Context/background:** Continues the healer dynamics of CASE-001/004, but about
**enforcement, exit, and reputation.** "Reroll" = abandon a character (often the
unwanted healer) to level a new one (often a DPS), i.e. an internal "quit" of the
role. "Public channels" = server-wide /trade or realm forums where reputation is made
or destroyed; "blacklisted on the server" = coordinated refusal to group/guild with a
named player, historically a real social sanction on a single realm.

## What it means, restated

Four linked mechanisms — the machinery for **controlling exit while keeping leadership's
hands clean**:

1. **Exit vilification.** Leaving is reframed as a *moral defect* ("disloyal, never
   belonged"), so the departed are branded, not mourned — and loyalty rhetoric is
   turned into a weapon against anyone who considers leaving.
2. **Outsourced (peer) policing.** Leadership runs a *campaign* to get rank-and-file
   raiders to apply the pressure, so surveillance and coercion of the burdened role
   become **horizontal** (peer-to-peer), not top-down.
3. **Deniable enforcement / proxies.** Leadership deliberately steps back from direct
   responsibility, installing **proxies** to absorb the rage — manufacturing
   **plausible deniability** so no leader "owns" it when someone critical quits.
4. **Reputational retaliation / blacklisting.** When the burdened role finally leaves
   and the group collapses, the leavers are **publicly blamed** and their reputation
   destroyed server-wide — forcing name changes or server transfers.

The through-line: **make the victims enforce the system on each other, and make exit
so costly (socially and reputationally) that the trap in CASE-004 has no escape hatch.**
