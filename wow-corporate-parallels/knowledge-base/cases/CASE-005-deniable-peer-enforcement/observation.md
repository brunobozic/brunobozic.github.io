# CASE-005 — Deniable Enforcement: Peer Policing, Exit Vilification & Blacklisting · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted · **Rating (from synthesis):** STRONG

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> And then there was the **vilification of people who quit / rerolled** as the worst
> kind of person — someone who didn't belong in the guild in the first place, because
> "we only need dedicated, loyal team players," and the others need to be driven out
> and burnt on a stake. It was a campaign directed at getting **other raiders to exert
> the pressure and police the healers**, so that at some point the pressure came mostly
> from *players*, not from guild leadership — because leadership seemingly learned they
> should not allow themselves to be directly involved or responsible when someone
> critical rerolls or quits. Instead they **installed proxies for the rage** when that
> happens, securing **plausible deniability** and a "shit umbrella."
>
> Also, when healers left and the raid group / guild disbanded, the **healers were
> blamed** for it — their role in bringing the guild down was **advertised in public
> channels**, so they had to change character names or be blacklisted on the server.

**Context/background:** Continues the healer dynamics of CASE-001/004, but about
**enforcement, exit, and reputation.** "Reroll" = abandon a character (often the
unwanted healer) to level a new one (often a DPS), i.e. an internal "quit" of the
role. "Public channels" = server-wide /trade or realm forums where reputation is made
or destroyed; "blacklisted on the server" = coordinated refusal to group/guild with a
named player, historically a real social sanction on a single realm.

## What it means, restated

Four linked mechanisms — the machinery for **controlling exit while keeping leadership's
hands clean**:

1. **Exit vilification.** Leaving is reframed as a *moral defect* ("disloyal, never
   belonged"), so the departed are branded, not mourned — and loyalty rhetoric is
   turned into a weapon against anyone who considers leaving.
2. **Outsourced (peer) policing.** Leadership runs a *campaign* to get rank-and-file
   raiders to apply the pressure, so surveillance and coercion of the burdened role
   become **horizontal** (peer-to-peer), not top-down.
3. **Deniable enforcement / proxies.** Leadership deliberately steps back from direct
   responsibility, installing **proxies** to absorb the rage — manufacturing
   **plausible deniability** so no leader "owns" it when someone critical quits.
4. **Reputational retaliation / blacklisting.** When the burdened role finally leaves
   and the group collapses, the leavers are **publicly blamed** and their reputation
   destroyed server-wide — forcing name changes or server transfers.

The through-line: **make the victims enforce the system on each other, and make exit
so costly (socially and reputationally) that the trap in CASE-004 has no escape hatch.**

## Independent WoW-community corroboration

**1. "Shaming gquitters" is a named phenomenon — and one leaders often route through a
proxy.** Scott Andrews' "Officers' Quarters" describes a struggling guild whose
mostly-absent GM delegates recruitment to a new officer, and it's the *officer* who
reacts aggressively when recruits quit — the leader off the record, the proxy
absorbing the fallout. Leaving is culturally coded inside guild discourse as a wound
to the group ("one of the most demoralizing events a guild must endure"), the
affective substrate loyalty/betrayal rhetoric exploits.
([Andrews — "Shaming gquitters"](https://www.yahoo.com/video/2013-11-25-officers-quarters-shaming-gquitters.html);
["An officer's guide to the /gquit"](https://www.engadget.com/2008-02-18-officers-quarters-the-gquit-an-officers-guide.html)) [OPINION]

**2. Reputational destruction → forced rename/transfer is a documented server dynamic.**
Community wikis describe how a "ninja" reputation gets a player progressively frozen
out of groups as word spreads, with **changing character name** as the escape hatch,
and **blacklists** kept by individuals/guilds. On low-population Classic/vanilla realms
"server reputation" carried real weight and could force a transfer off. This is the
exact machinery of mechanism 4 — here originally aimed at *thieves*, which is why
turning it on *leavers* is the abuse.
([Vanilla WoW Wiki — Loot ninja](https://vanilla-wow-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Loot_ninja);
[Blacklist](https://vanilla-wow-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Blacklist);
[Blizzard Classic forums — blacklisted players](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/keeping-track-of-blacklisted-players-in-classic/159072)) [OPINION]

**3. Control in WoW is bottom-up, tool-mediated, and embraced by the policed.** T.L.
Taylor's ethnography documents player-based regulation and **raid-surveillance addons**
(e.g., CT_RaidAssist) that let leaders monitor players — surveillance normalized because
it aids play. That's *how* coercion becomes horizontal and deniable: the policed adopt
the tools themselves.
([Taylor 2006, "Does WoW Change Everything?", *Games and Culture* 1(4)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1555412006292615)) [EVIDENCE]

**4. Peer/reputation norms are, at baseline, cooperative — which is what makes their
weaponization possible.** MMO reciprocity/prosocial research shows helping and
reputation-management sustain a healthy social system; leaders exploit that existing
machinery when they steer it against specific burdened players.
([Group Play and Reciprocity, *Computers in Human Behavior* 2022](http://dmitriwilliams.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Group-Play-and-Reciprocity-CHB-2022.pdf)) [EVIDENCE]

## What corroborates / what complicates

- **Strongly supports:** exit-shaming as a named pattern; the leader-uses-a-proxy
  structure; the reputation-destruction → rename/transfer pathway; and the horizontal,
  tool-mediated, self-adopted nature of guild surveillance.
- **First-hand / thinner:** the *deliberate campaign* to make peers police healers, and
  leadership's *strategic* installation of proxies for plausible deniability, is the
  author's testimony. The *ingredients* are all documented; the *orchestration* is the
  observation's reading. Flagged `[FIRST-HAND]` in `sources.md`.
- **Complicates (kept honestly):** (a) **blacklists began as legitimate defense**
  against real ninja-looters/scammers — the wrong is the *category error* of aiming a
  thief-defense tool at a leaver. (b) **Peer norms can be genuine self-governance**, not
  coercion. (c) Notably, the guild-leadership **advice literature itself largely
  condemns** shaming quitters and raider-shaming — so this is a recognized *abuse*, not
  a community-endorsed norm. (d) Roster churn is often mundane housekeeping, not
  vilification.
