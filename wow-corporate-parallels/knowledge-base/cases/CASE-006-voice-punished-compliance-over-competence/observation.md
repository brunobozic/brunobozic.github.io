# CASE-006 — Naming the Problem: Punished Voice & Compliance Over Competence · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted (research pass in progress) · **Rating (from synthesis):** TBD

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> At one point healers complained that this had become an **unpaid job** — with all
> the pressure to attend, with all the time needed to get potions and flasks — and
> that working for the so-called mutual good **5–6 hours per day for no reward** is
> not what a computer game was all about.
>
> They were labelled as **troublesome and difficult to work with, immature and
> selfish** — which also reminds me of how in corporate it's worse being the one who
> says what's wrong than being the top performer. If you are causing trouble, you're
> out — no matter if you're crucial. **Compliance being more important than
> performance.**

**Context/background:** Follows directly from CASE-001/004/005. The healers here do
something the earlier cases hadn't yet centered: they **articulate the exploitation
accurately** — unpaid labor, coerced attendance, self-funded prep grind, no reward —
and are punished not for *underperforming* but for **saying it**. "Potions and flasks"
= the self-farmed raid consumables (CASE-001). "5–6 hrs/day" = the raid + prep time
sink.

## What it means, restated

Two linked claims — and the second is the novel one:

1. **Accurate naming of exploitation.** The burdened workers correctly diagnose their
   situation ("this is an unpaid second job with no reward") — a *legitimate grievance*,
   not a performance failure.
2. **Voice is punished; compliance beats competence.** Rather than address the
   grievance, leadership attacks the *messenger* — recoding a legitimate complaint as a
   **character defect** ("troublesome, immature, selfish"). The lesson the org teaches:
   **naming the problem is a worse offense than any performance problem, and being
   crucial doesn't protect you — compliance does.** The truth-teller is managed out.

The through-line with prior cases: CASE-005 made *leaving* costly; CASE-006 makes
*speaking up* costly — closing the last valve. The only sanctioned options left are
silent compliance or a punished exit.
