## The three most on-point anchors (lead with these)

1. **Burris (2012), *AMJ*** — managers rate **challenging** voice as **worse
   performance** than **supportive** voice *at equal idea quality*, via perceived
   loyalty/threat. "Compliance is scored as competence." The devastating one-liner.
2. **John, Blunden & Liu (2019), "Shooting the Messenger," *JEP:G*** — 11 experiments;
   **blameless** bearers of bad news are disliked and penalized, distinct from
   disagreeing with the content. "Being right doesn't protect the namer."
3. **Milliken, Morrison & Hewlin (2003), *JMS*** — the **#1 reason for silence is fear
   of the troublemaker/complainer label** — the recoding, anticipated in advance.

## Why this case matters to the argument

- **It closes the system.** CASE-004 traps the worker in role; CASE-005 welds the exit
  shut; **CASE-006 punishes speaking up** — so the last valve (voice) is closed. After
  it, the burdened worker has only: **silent compliance, punished exit, or punished
  protest.** The enclosure is complete.
- **Powered by CASE-003's engine, continuous with CASE-005's:** discrediting the namer
  and recoding the grievance is **self-/reputation-protection under weak accountability**
  — do-gooder derogation and DARVO are the same self-serving-attribution / moral-
  disengagement family.
- **Cleanest quotable results in the project:** "challenging voice literally lowers your
  performance rating" (Burris); "people punish blameless messengers" (John et al.).

## The sharp reconciliation (do not drop — it makes the case earned)

Naive "compliance is always rewarded" is **false**: Judge, Livingston & Hurst (2012)
find *disagreeable* men out-earn agreeable ones (~18%). The resolution sharpens the
whole thesis: **self-promoting disagreeableness aimed upward / for oneself is often
rewarded; challenging voice aimed at the organization's own practices is punished.**
The rule isn't "don't be difficult" — it's **"don't name *our* problems."**

## CONTRADICTS / COMPLICATES (the boundary — see `synthesis.md`)

- Not all voice is constructive (some is corrosive/bad-faith venting) — the claim is
  about **accurate, good-faith, improvement-oriented** voice.
- Teams genuinely need cooperative norms; the failure is when **fit displaces
  competence** (Rivera), not when it accompanies it.
- Some dissent is wrong on the merits; Burris holds *controlling for idea quality*,
  which real orgs can't cleanly observe.
- **Many orgs *do* value voice** (Edmondson; Project Aristotle) — this is a **failure
  mode, not an iron law**; the cross-org variance is the point.
- WoW twin of the boundary: "no bad players, only bad guild matches" — some "drama" is
  fit-mismatch, and hardcore raiding is opt-in.
