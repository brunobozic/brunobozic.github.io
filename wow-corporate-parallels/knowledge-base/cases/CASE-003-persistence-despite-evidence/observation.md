# CASE-003 — Same Brain: Persistence Despite Disconfirming Evidence · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted (research pass in progress) · **Rating (from synthesis):** TBD

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> What I find amusing is that ADHD, narcissistic, 9-year-old guild leaders think and
> strategize the same way as grown C-level executives — even when pure logic and
> observable results tell them that what they are doing is not producing results.

**Context/background:** Meta-observation drawn from 15+ years of guild leadership,
sitting *above* the specific mechanics of CASE-001 (coercion) and CASE-002
(gatekeeping). "9-year-old" is used figuratively for immaturity/impulsivity, though
some guild leaders were in fact very young; the point is the **striking isomorphism
of leadership cognition** across a huge gulf in age, maturity, stakes, and training.

## What it means, restated

Two linked claims:

1. **Isomorphism of leadership cognition.** People occupying the "top" of a small
   voluntary hierarchy (a guild) exhibit the *same* strategic reasoning patterns,
   ego-defenses, and blind spots as people at the top of large formal organizations
   (the C-suite) — despite wildly different age, maturity, competence, and stakes.
2. **Persistence despite disconfirming evidence.** Both keep doing the thing that
   demonstrably isn't working — the wipes keep happening, the metrics keep sliding —
   and continue anyway, as if observable results and pure logic simply don't reach
   the decision.

The *interesting* implication (to be developed in synthesis): if the cognition is
independent of the person's age/maturity/skill, then it is likely produced by the
**structural position** — unaccountable or lightly-accountable authority, plus
public identity investment in past decisions — rather than by the individual. The
guild leader and the CEO converge because the *seat* shapes the thinking.
