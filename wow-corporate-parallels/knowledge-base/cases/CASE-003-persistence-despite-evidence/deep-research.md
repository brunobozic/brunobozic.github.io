## The one-paragraph synthesis (from the research)

Escalation/self-justification is a **universal human default** that intensifies with
**personal responsibility for the prior decision** (Staw 1976; Sleesman et al. 2012
meta-analysis) — an identity variable the *position* supplies. **Power itself**
shifts cognition toward reward-focused, disinhibited, feedback-discounting
processing (Keltner et al. 2003) and, held long and unconstrained, may harden into
**acquired hubris** (Owen & Davidson 2009). Ordinary **confirmation bias** and
**self-serving attribution** (Nickerson 1998; Staw/McKechnie/Puffer 1983) then let
the powerholder not *see* — or explain away — disconfirming results, while
**groupthink** dynamics (Janis) remove the messengers. Personality (narcissism,
overconfidence) modulates intensity (Chatterjee & Hambrick 2007; Roll 1986;
Malmendier & Tate 2005) but, per **Grijalva et al. (2015)**, cannot carry the
explanation alone. The honest boundary conditions — de-escalation is achievable
(Sivanathan et al. 2008), persistence is sometimes correct (Duckworth et al. 2007),
and effects shrink under real accountability (Hayward & Hambrick 1997) — convert a
blanket claim into a defensible one: **the guild leader and the CEO reason alike to
the exact extent that both occupy unaccountable seats they have publicly staked
their identity on.**

## What each track added

**WoW side (practitioner + game-studies):**
- A recognizable **dysfunctional-GM type**, cataloged across Scott Andrews'
  decade-long "Officers' Quarters" column and *The Guild Leader's Handbook*:
  self-dealing loot rage, surveillance/expulsion, guild-as-ego-extension.
- The **structural-power point, from the academy**: GM authority is near-absolute
  and guild survival hinges on it (Poor & Skoric 2014; *Massively OP* 2023);
  baseline guild fragility (~25% die in a month — Ducheneaut et al. 2007).
- The community's **own rejection of the age explanation** ("age ≠ maturity" —
  MMO-Champion), relocating the problem to *disposition under authority* — which is
  the observation's structural thesis in the players' own words.

**Corporate side (management + psychology):** five evidenced mechanisms —
escalation of commitment (A), CEO narcissism (B), hubris/overconfidence (C),
upper-echelons + power's cognitive effects (D), and motivated reasoning/groupthink
(E). Two moderators recur and *are* the structural variables the observation names:
**(1) personal responsibility for the prior decision** and **(2) weak accountability.**

## The load-bearing four (cite these first)

1. **Sleesman et al. (2012)** — meta-analytic proof that personal responsibility
   amplifies escalation. (The "why they double down" backbone.)
2. **Keltner et al. (2003)** — power itself degrades feedback processing in *any*
   powerholder. (The "same organism under the same lever" mechanism.)
3. **Hayward & Hambrick (1997)** — hubris effects are **worse under weak oversight**.
   (Operationalizes "unaccountable seat" as a measured moderator.)
4. **Grijalva et al. (2015)** — narcissism predicts *emergence*, not *effectiveness*.
   (The clincher for **selection + structure over personality** — and the reason the
   "9-year-old ≈ CEO" observation works.)

## CONTRADICTS / COMPLICATES (the scope condition — see `synthesis.md`)

- **Selection/survivorship bias** — failure writing over-samples villains; adaptive/
  competent leaders exist and are under-narrated (Bronze League GM; "good GM"
  literature). Guild death is often **burnout/structural churn**, not a villain
  (Andrews "burned out leader"; Spinks; Ducheneaut fragility).
- **Persistence is sometimes rational** (Duckworth grit); real disconfirming
  evidence is usually noisy, not the clean "pure logic" the observation invokes.
- **De-escalation is achievable** (Sivanathan et al. 2008) → behavior is an
  identity-threat response, not destiny.
- **Accountability blunts it** → the parallel is weakest where governance bites; the
  CEO is not *fully* unaccountable.
- **Base rate** → most executives aren't clinical narcissists; the claim is a
  *structure-produced failure mode in susceptible individuals*, not "leaders are
  narcissists."
