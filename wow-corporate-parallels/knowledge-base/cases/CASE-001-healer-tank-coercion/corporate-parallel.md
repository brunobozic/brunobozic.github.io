# CASE-001 — The Healer/Tank Coercion Cycle · Corporate Parallel

> **Status:** drafted

## The corporate/organizational equivalent

An organization depends on a **scarce, high-burden, structurally under-rewarded
role** — the unglamorous "load-bearing" people (the on-call SRE, the maintainer
of the legacy system, the senior nurse, the one person who actually knows the
payroll pipeline). Instead of making the role *attractive* (pay, recognition,
lighter load, real career upside), management leans on **coercion and loyalty
rhetoric**: mandatory availability, "we're a family," "go the extra mile,"
extra scrutiny, and unpaid preparation — while the people setting these rules
exempt themselves and capture the scarce rewards. The predictable outcome is
burnout and a retention collapse that no amount of exhortation reverses; only a
change to the *incentive system* fixes it.

Five mechanisms in the observation map onto documented corporate dynamics:

## 1. "Family / loyalty / duty / the extra mile" as extraction rhetoric

- The "we are a family" framing is widely identified as a corporate red flag: it
  "tries to access your emotional side, not just your professional one," so
  employees "stay silent, adjust, overwork, and endure unfair situations."
  ([Entrepreneur — "Don't fall for the 'we're a family' trap"](https://www.entrepreneur.com/leadership/dont-fall-for-the-were-a-family-trap-in-the/433771);
  [Medium — "Why 'We Are a Family' Is the Biggest Corporate Red Flag"](https://medium.com/@simrangautam2551/why-we-are-a-family-is-the-biggest-corporate-red-flag-8f8a90f21288)).
  *Opinion/commentary, but broadly consistent.*
- Peer-adjacent research: a study covered by ASU's W.P. Carey School finds that
  **loyal employees are perceived as more exploitable** and are preferentially
  targeted for extra, uncompensated burdens — a "vicious circle" where loyalty
  invites more exploitation, which is exactly the "duty/extra mile" trap applied
  to the raider who "loyally" keeps healing.
  ([ASU W.P. Carey — "Company loyalty leads to exploitation and unpaid work"](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20230321-company-loyalty-leads-exploitation-and-unpaid-work-study)).
  *Evidence: academic study (Management-side research).*

## 2. Punishing/coercing the scarce role instead of rewarding it → attrition
("performance punishment")

- The "performance punishment" cycle: reliable high performers get **more work as
  the reward for good work**, without more pay or recognition, producing burnout
  and turnover. This is the healer's fate — competence in a needed role is repaid
  with lock-in and obligation, not upside.
  ([EWF International — "Overcoming the Performance Punishment Cycle"](https://ewfinternational.com/overcoming-performance-punishment-cycle/);
  [TeamBonding — "Performance Punishment"](https://www.teambonding.com/performance-punishment/)).
  *Opinion/practitioner literature.*
- Hard evidence for the retention collapse: a peer-reviewed *Management Science*
  study ("Cut to the Curve") finds employees who are under-recognized/downgraded
  are **~34% more likely to voluntarily exit** — even when compensated with larger
  bonuses — because recognition, once made scarce, drives behavior more than money.
  Mirrors "people left promptly to join a guild that *did* raid."
  ([Management Science — "Cut to the Curve: Underrecognition and Talent Loss from Forced Ranking"](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.01204)).
  *Evidence: peer-reviewed study.*

## 3. The managerial-caste double standard (asymmetric scrutiny; rules for thee)

- The author's "gear inspections for healers/tanks but not DPS, and leaders exempt
  themselves" is the classic leadership double standard: "setting standards they
  don't follow, exempting themselves from rules they impose on others." It is
  repeatedly named as a top trust-destroyer, after which "the best performers
  eventually disengage" and "teams begin to protect themselves instead of
  performing."
  ([Forbes — "The 'Do As I Say' Leader: Why Hypocrisy Kills Leadership"](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbescoachescouncil/2026/04/07/the-do-as-i-say-leader-why-hypocrisy-kills-leadership/);
  [Zenger Folkman — "Trust Breakers: 8 Leadership Mistakes"](https://zengerfolkman.com/articles/trust-breakers-8-leadership-mistakes-that-destroy-team-confidence/)).
  *Opinion/leadership research.*

## 4. Unpaid preparation grind (the flasks/elixirs = after-hours prep)

- Requiring raiders to self-farm expensive consumables as a precondition to
  participate parallels the uncompensated "invisible work" and off-the-clock prep
  loaded disproportionately onto certain roles — the same loyalty-exploitation
  mechanism the ASU study describes (extra unpaid work flows to those who won't
  refuse).
  ([ASU W.P. Carey study, as above](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20230321-company-loyalty-leads-exploitation-and-unpaid-work-study)).
  *Evidence + interpretation.*

## 5. Hoarding the scarce rewards at the top (loot for leaders/trustees)

- Leaders reserving the best PvP drops for themselves and a trusted inner circle
  maps to reward capture by an in-group — bonuses, promotions, plum projects, and
  recognition concentrating among leadership and favorites. This corrodes the
  perceived fairness that holds a meritocracy together: "nothing destroys
  meritocracy faster than visible double standards."
  ([Forbes double-standard article, as above](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbescoachescouncil/2026/04/07/the-do-as-i-say-leader-why-hypocrisy-kills-leadership/)).
  *Opinion/leadership research.*

## How the dynamic operates at work

The corporate version runs on the same loop as the guild:

1. A role is essential but under-rewarded relative to its burden.
2. Supply of willing people dries up.
3. Leadership treats the shortage as a **compliance problem** (mandates, loyalty
   talk, scrutiny) rather than an **incentive problem** (pay, recognition, load).
4. The people in the role feel exploited; the most capable — who have the easiest
   exit — leave first.
5. Collapse or chronic dysfunction persists until the incentive *system* changes,
   not until leaders try harder.

## What corroborates / what complicates

- **Supports the parallel:** all five mechanisms have real corporate analogues,
  two backed by academic evidence (loyalty→exploitation; under-recognition→exit).
- **Complicates it:** in a company, pay is a real lever guild leaders lacked — a
  firm *can* simply pay more for the hard role, so corporate coercion is more
  clearly a *choice* than in a guild where leaders had no salary to offer. This
  makes the corporate version arguably *worse*, not merely analogous.
- **Gaps:** the "loot hoarding by leaders" → "reward capture by leadership"
  mapping is well-argued but rests on leadership-commentary sources rather than a
  single clean dataset; treat as strong-argument rather than proven-effect.
