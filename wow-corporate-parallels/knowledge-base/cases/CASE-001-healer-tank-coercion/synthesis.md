# CASE-001 — The Healer/Tank Coercion Cycle · Synthesis

> **Rating:** STRONG

## The parallel in one sentence

In a raiding guild, a scarce, under-rewarded role (healers, then tanks) was met
with coercion, loyalty rhetoric, and asymmetric scrutiny instead of better
incentives — and in a company the same scarce load-bearing roles get "we're a
family," extra unpaid burden, and a managerial double standard instead of pay and
recognition; both are the same mistake: **treating an incentive problem as a
compliance problem**, and both end in the same place — the capable leave and the
system collapses until the *incentives*, not the exhortations, change.

## The shared mechanism

When a role's **burden exceeds its rewards**, willing supply collapses.
Leadership then faces a fork:

- **Fix the incentives** (make the role desirable: reward, recognition, lighter
  load, upside), or
- **Force compliance** (mandates, loyalty/duty rhetoric, surveillance, lock-in,
  and reserving rewards for the in-group).

Coercion is cheaper and faster *in the short run* and flatters leaders who'd
rather not give anything up — so under pressure, weak leadership reliably chooses
it. But coercion attacks the symptom (attendance) while worsening the cause
(the role is now even less rewarding). The most capable people have the lowest
exit cost, so they leave first, which raises the burden on those remaining, which
accelerates the exodus. The system does not stabilize through willpower; it
stabilizes only when the **underlying reward structure** changes — Blizzard
re-itemizing/buffing hybrids and reworking tanking; a company actually paying and
promoting for the hard role.

The rhetoric is identical because it does identical work: "family, loyalty, duty,
the extra mile" reframes an **extractive ask as a moral one**, so refusing feels
like betrayal rather than a rational response to bad terms. Research on
loyalty-as-exploitability shows this isn't a metaphor — loyal people are
*measurably* targeted for more unpaid burden.

## Where it holds

- Scarce, essential, high-burden role that others avoid. ✔ (healer/tank ≈ on-call,
  legacy maintainer, senior nurse)
- Leadership responds with control + loyalty rhetoric rather than incentives. ✔
- Asymmetric scrutiny: the burdened caste is inspected; the comfortable caste
  isn't; leaders exempt themselves. ✔ (gear inspections/flask checks ≈
  monitoring the ICs while management is unaccountable)
- Reward capture by the in-group. ✔ (loot for leaders/trustees ≈ bonuses/
  promotions concentrating at the top)
- Resolution only via systemic incentive change. ✔ (Blizzard patch ≈ comp/
  structure reform, not a pep talk)

## Where it breaks (disanalogies)

- **Pay exists at work; it didn't in the guild.** A company *can* just pay more
  for the hard role — guild leaders had no salary lever, only social ones. This
  cuts two ways: it means the analogy isn't perfect, but it also makes corporate
  coercion **more culpable**, since a real fix was available and declined.
- **Exit cost differs by orders of magnitude.** Leaving a guild costs a night's
  annoyance; leaving a job risks livelihood. So corporate coercion can persist
  *longer* than guild coercion precisely because people can't walk as easily —
  the guild's fast collapse is the *compressed, honest* version of a slower
  corporate rot.
- **Voluntariness.** Raiding is a hobby; work is survival. The guild surfaces the
  dynamic in weeks because there's nothing holding people but the terms
  themselves — which is exactly why it's such a clean laboratory for it.

## Rating justification

**STRONG.** The mechanism match is tight and runs end-to-end (scarcity →
coercion-vs-incentive fork → loyalty rhetoric → asymmetric scrutiny → reward
capture → capable-first attrition → systemic-only resolution). Both sides are
well sourced, including two pieces of academic evidence on the corporate side
(loyalty→exploitation; under-recognition→~34% higher voluntary exit). The
disanalogies (pay, exit cost, voluntariness) don't break the parallel — they
explain why the guild version is *faster and clearer*, which is the whole thesis
of the project.
