# CASE-001 — The Healer/Tank Coercion Cycle · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted · **Rating (from synthesis):** STRONG

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> Healers in Vanilla WoW were in a bad position in PvP — couldn't do damage,
> couldn't progress solo — so not many people played them. Guilds then had
> trouble raiding: no healers, no raid. Instead of *rewarding* healers, guild
> leadership reached for coercion — Dragon Kill Points (DKP) and later
> attendance requirements to force them to show up. The funny thing is most
> guild leaders themselves rolled healers but *played* DPS because it was more
> rewarding, while having no problem forcing others to heal for them.
>
> It got worse. Hybrid healers were mandated into a single spec, and — to make
> it "more fun" — they could no longer roll on DPS gear, only healer gear,
> slowly pigeonholing them into the healer role. Valuable PvP drops were kept
> for guild leaders and their trustees. The hybrid class in question was the
> **druid**. Later the same thing happened to **tanks** — same story: instead
> of incentivizing tank players, leaders chose to punish and manipulate.
>
> The big words were **family, loyalty, duty, the extra mile** — which deeply
> reminds me of corporate narrative.
>
> At the peak of these issues, guilds were falling apart left and right,
> friendships ruined — and it did not stop until Blizzard fixed the system for
> healers, and later tanks. At the peak, healers and tanks had gear
> checkups/inspects before raid (elitism) and had to present flasks and elixirs
> they'd grinded on their own; DPS classes did not get scrutinized — which
> reminds me of a managerial caste in corporate. Guilds fell apart overnight:
> healers logged in on their DPS alts, nobody raided, and people left promptly
> to join a guild that *did* raid. This madness continued for months, across all
> servers and guilds. We all knew what the problem was, but guild leadership was
> unable to pivot.

**Context/background:** Vanilla / Classic WoW era (2004–2006, patch 1.x),
40-person raids (Molten Core, Blackwing Lair, AQ40, Naxxramas). Author writing
from 15+ years of guild-leadership experience. Roles referenced: Guild Master
(GM), officers/"trustees," raid healers, main tanks, DPS.

## What it means, restated

A voluntary organization depended on a **scarce, structurally under-rewarded
role** (healers; later tanks). Rather than fix the incentives so people *wanted*
the role, leadership escalated **coercion and control** — mandatory attendance,
spec lock-in, loot restrictions that trapped people in the role, self-farmed
consumable requirements, and pre-raid inspections applied unequally. Leaders
exempted themselves from the very burdens they imposed and hoarded the scarce
rewards. The rhetoric of "family/loyalty/duty" was used to justify it. The result
was mass attrition and organizational collapse that persisted until the *system's
underlying incentives* were fixed — not the leadership's willpower.

## Independent WoW-community corroboration

**1. The healer (and tank) shortage is a real, persistent, structural role problem —
not just this author's guild.**
- Community and forum discussion consistently describes group-role imbalance: an
  oversupply of DPS and a chronic shortage of healers/tanks across the game's
  history. ([Blizzard EU forums — "Healer shortage"](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/healer-shortage/490265);
  [Guilds of WoW — "The Healer problem"](https://guildsofwow.com/healer-problem/players)).
  *Evidence/opinion: community opinion + widely-shared lived experience.*

**2. Non-healing druids were effectively unwelcome in Vanilla raids — the
pigeonholing was structural.**
- Retrospective guides state plainly that "historically, there was no place for
  non-Resto druids in end-game raiding in Vanilla WoW," and that poor itemization
  was a major reason hybrids suffered in early raids — i.e., feral/balance druids
  were pushed into restoration whether they wanted it or not.
  ([Vanilla WoW Wiki — Druid PvE guide](https://vanilla-wow-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Druid_PvE_guide);
  [Nirklars — Vanilla WoW Druid guide](https://nirklars.wordpress.com/wow/vanilla-wow-druid/)).
  *Evidence/opinion: community guide consensus.*

**3. The "hybrid tax" was a real, named design philosophy — hybrids were
deliberately kept weaker, and Blizzard later walked it back.**
- The "hybrid tax" is the idea that classes able to fill multiple roles should be
  penalized in raw output relative to "pure" (single-role) classes. It was a
  popular community argument that Blizzard eventually **denounced**, spreading
  hybrid utility more evenly in *Wrath of the Lich King* — corroborating the
  author's point that the situation only resolved when **Blizzard changed the
  system**, not when leaders changed their minds.
  ([MMORPG.com — "The Pures vs Hybrids Debate"](https://www.mmorpg.com/general-articles/the-pures-vs-hybrids-debate-2000116808);
  [Blue Tracker — "Hybrid tax"](https://www.bluetracker.gg/wow/topic/us-en/20677330431-hybrid-tax/)).
  *Evidence: developer-sourced design history.*

**4. DKP and attendance were explicitly attendance-forcing mechanisms.**
- DKP ("Dragon Kill Points") awards points for **attending** guild raids, spent to
  bid on loot; guilds layered on attendance rules and bonus points to reward
  showing up. Sources note DKP "incentivizes consistent raid attendance." This is
  exactly the author's point: the lever pulled was *attendance compliance*, not
  *role desirability*. (Origin: EverQuest, "Thott of Afterlife," named after the
  only two raid dragons at the time.)
  ([Wowpedia — Dragon kill points](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_kill_points);
  [Wikipedia — Dragon kill points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_kill_points)).
  *Evidence: primary/reference documentation.*

**5. Self-farmed consumables (flasks/elixirs) were a real, expensive, role-loaded
raiding tax.**
- Healer/tank consumable guides show flasks and elixirs were costly, player-farmed,
  and could matter "more than a full tier of gear" — a substantial unpaid grind
  borne by the raider, exactly as described. Note: several sources say healer
  consumable pressure varied by guild and progression, which slightly tempers the
  "always mandatory" framing.
  ([Wowhead — Priest Healing Consumables](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/guide/classes/priest/healer-consumables-raid-buffs-pve);
  [Icy Veins — Priest Healer consumables](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-classic/priest-healer-pve-enchants-consumables)).
  *Evidence: guide documentation.*

## What corroborates / what complicates

- **Strongly supports:** the role shortage, the structural pigeonholing of
  druids, the named "hybrid tax," DKP/attendance as compliance levers, the
  expensive self-farmed consumable grind, and — critically — that the problem
  resolved when **Blizzard changed the system**, not through leadership.
- **First-hand, not independently verified (plausible, consistent with known
  loot-council abuse debates):** the specific corruption claims — leaders keeping
  valuable PvP drops for themselves and "trustees," and leaders rolling healer
  characters but playing DPS while forcing others to heal. These match
  well-documented *categories* of guild drama (loot favoritism, officer
  privilege) but this exact pattern is the author's testimony. Marked
  `[FIRST-HAND]` in `sources.md`.
- **Complicates:** consumable/inspection strictness varied by guild and by
  progression phase; it was not uniformly enforced everywhere. The author's
  account describes the *harsh end* of a real spectrum.
- **Gaps:** we lack a single tidy write-up of "guilds collapsed overnight when
  healers logged onto DPS alts." Forum threads reference guild collapse from
  role imbalance and drama generally, but not this precise mechanism — it reads
  as a true, common lived pattern that was rarely documented formally.
