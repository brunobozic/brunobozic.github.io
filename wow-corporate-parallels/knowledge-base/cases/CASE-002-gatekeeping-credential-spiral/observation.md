# CASE-002 — The Gatekeeping / Credential Spiral · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted · **Rating (from synthesis):** STRONG

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> After a certain point in an expansion, your gear was examined and if it was
> too low you were not allowed to raid — **even if the gear level was OK by
> Blizzard's own requirements**. Leadership wanted to farm epics from end-game
> instances at a quick pace, so they selected people who were already
> well-geared. This escalated: you ended up needing gear *equal to or better
> than what the raid itself dropped*, which made no sense to anyone but
> leadership.
>
> It reminds me of 6–7 rounds of interviews in IT, whereas management gets
> hired over a dinner — "I don't need to know programming, I have people under
> me who need to know that stuff."
>
> This utter nonsense persisted, and in some ways still persists today, showing
> that reason and objective reality are not what drive leadership — more the
> internal will to bend rules and "optimize" their own growth.

**Context/background:** Later Vanilla through Wrath-of-the-Lich-King era; the
gatekeeping intensified once the **GearScore** addon (WotLK, ~2009) let people
put a single number on a stranger's gear and reject them on sight. "Item level"
= Blizzard's own gear metric; "GearScore" (GS) = a community addon that
aggregated it. The core absurdity: being denied entry to the content that
*produces* the gear, for not already having the gear.

## What it means, restated

An **entry requirement inflated past the point of function**: the bar to be let
in rose above what the activity objectively needed — and above what the activity
itself would grant you — so the only way to qualify was to have *already* cleared
it elsewhere. A self-serving elite (leaders wanting fast, safe farm runs)
optimized for their own convenience, producing a **catch-22** that locked out
competent newcomers. Meanwhile the people *setting* the bar were not themselves
subject to an equivalent one.

## Independent WoW-community corroboration

**1. GearScore gatekeeping demanding more than the content dropped is
well-documented.**
- Community accounts describe groups "setting extremely high gear requirements …
  needing much higher GearScore than what the current raid content actually
  dropped" — the exact "you need better gear than the raid gives" absurdity the
  author names.
  ([Warmane forums — "The insanity of GearScore"](https://forum.warmane.com/showthread.php?t=312234);
  [Blizzard EU forums — "Raid gear GS gatekeeping and solution for it"](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/raid-gear-gs-gatekeeping-and-solution-for-it/565629)).
  *Opinion/community, but consistent and widespread.*

**2. It was explicitly recognized as a catch-22 at the time.**
- A 2009 blog is literally titled **"Gear Score, WoW's Catch-22: A Fail
  Concept"**, articulating: to get gear you need a good GearScore, but you need a
  good GearScore to be let into the raid that drops the gear. PUG leaders also
  demanded you **link the achievement proving you'd already cleared the
  instance** — a second, redundant barrier.
  ([Storm, Earth, and Fire — "Gear Score, WoW's Catch-22"](https://stormearthandfire.wordpress.com/2009/10/13/gear-score-wows-catch-22-a-fail-concept/)).
  *Opinion (contemporaneous primary account).*

**3. GearScore was a poor proxy — the metric didn't measure what mattered.**
- Community consensus notes "many things that improve raiding performance gave no
  GearScore points," yet people rated others by the number anyway. A high bar that
  doesn't even track real capability = credentialism, not competence-screening —
  matching the author's "makes no sense to anyone but leadership."
  ([MMO-Champion — GearScore/RaiderIO discussion](https://www.mmo-champion.com/threads/2562744-Raider-IO-needs-to-go-It-s-just-as-bad-for-the-community-as-Gearscore-was-in-WOTLK/page16)).
  *Opinion/community.*

**4. The pattern recurred (RaiderIO today), i.e. "in some ways still persists."**
- The same community explicitly frames today's RaiderIO score gatekeeping as the
  successor to WotLK GearScore — supporting the author's "still persists today."
  ([MMO-Champion — "RaiderIO … just as bad … as Gearscore was in WotLK"](https://www.mmo-champion.com/threads/2562744-Raider-IO-needs-to-go-It-s-just-as-bad-for-the-community-as-Gearscore-was-in-WOTLK/page16)).
  *Opinion/community.*

## What corroborates / what complicates

- **Strongly supports:** the inflated bar (higher GS than the content drops), the
  catch-22 structure, the "prove you already did it" achievement-link barrier, the
  metric being a poor proxy for real skill, and the persistence of the pattern
  into the present (RaiderIO).
- **First-hand, plausible:** the specific *motive* attributed to leadership
  (wanting fast epic farm runs, so selecting the already-geared). This is a
  reasonable and common reading, well-aligned with documented PUG behavior, but
  the intent is the author's inference. Marked `[FIRST-HAND]` in `sources.md`.
- **Complicates:** some gatekeeping had a *kernel* of rationality — a floor of
  gear does reduce wipe risk. The absurdity is in the **inflation past function**
  and the **catch-22**, not in having any bar at all. Keep that distinction.
- **Gaps:** the tight "management is exempt from an equivalent bar" comparison
  lives on the *corporate* side of this case; within WoW the analog is softer
  (leaders set the bar but were already geared), so the two-castes point is
  carried mostly by the corporate parallel.
