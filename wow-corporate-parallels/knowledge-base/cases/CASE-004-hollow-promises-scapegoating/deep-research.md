## The three heaviest anchors (lead with these)

1. **Zhao, Wayne, Glibkowski & Bravo (2007), *Personnel Psychology*** — meta-analytic
   proof that broken promises degrade commitment/performance and raise turnover,
   *mediated by the felt sense of betrayal.* (The deferral mechanism.)
2. **Haegele, "Talent Hoarding in Organizations" (arXiv)** — a natural experiment in
   which reducing hoarding raised promotion applications **~123%**, with ~75% of
   managers self-reporting hoarding. Causal evidence for "we can't afford to lose a
   healer." (Working paper — confirm peer-review status.)
3. **Fast & Tiedens (2010), "Blame Contagion," *JESP*** — experiments showing
   blame-shifting *spreads* and is driven by self-image protection. (The scapegoating
   mechanism — and the bridge to CASE-003's self-serving attribution.)

## Why this case matters to the argument

- It's the **career-and-blame face of CASE-001**: the burdened role gets it *coming*
  (coerced in) and *going* (trapped in place, then blamed).
- It's powered by **CASE-003's engine**: hollow promises and scapegoating are
  self-justification / reputation-protection under weak accountability. Blame
  contagion (Fast & Tiedens) is literally the same ego-defense mechanism.
- It gives the article a rare **emotional + empirical** one-two: the gut-punch of
  "lazy healers" and "we'll see next season," paired with a hard number (**~123%**
  suppressed promotion applications) that proves the trap is real at work.

## CONTRADICTS / COMPLICATES (the boundary — see `synthesis.md`)

The case is credible only as an indictment of **dishonesty, non-delivery, and
cause-blind blame**, not of role assignment, retention, standards, or accountability:

- Breach is often **inadvertent** (frozen headcount, scarce slots); harm tracks
  *perceived intent + communication* — *breach* vs. felt *violation*.
- Some **retention-in-role is legitimate**; hoarding is the *incentive-driven private
  suppression* of options, not all in-place retention.
- **PIPs/trials can be genuine** used early and transparently; misuse ≠ the tool.
- **Individual accountability is sometimes right** — "just culture" *balances* learning
  with accountability; some wipes really are healing failures. The wrong is
  *defaulting* to the person when the cause is systemic.
- Scapegoating isn't healer-exclusive (tanks catch it too) — the dynamic is "blame the
  visible responsibility-role."
