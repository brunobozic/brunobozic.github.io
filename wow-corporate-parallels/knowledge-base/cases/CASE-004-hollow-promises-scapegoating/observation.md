# CASE-004 — The Trap: Hollow Promises, Talent-Hoarding & Scapegoating · Observation (WoW side)

> **Status:** drafted (research pass in progress) · **Rating (from synthesis):** TBD

## The observation (author, first-hand)

> When they promised being allowed to roll on DPS gear and advance through the
> guild, they were just buying time — simply lying about the prospects of any
> advancement, focused on keeping the engine that feeds them running at optimal
> speed.
>
> Even when metrics showed you were as good a DPS as the other DPS, you were still
> kept as a healer because they could not afford to lose a healer.
>
> Rewards, promotions, etc. never came — it was always "we'll see next season."
> Kinda the same as in corporate.
>
> Guilds even had a "**performance improvement plan**" to keep people who wanted to
> advance through the ranks stuck at the position that is most detrimental for them.
>
> Also, at each wipe it was always the healers' fault — "learn to heal," "lazy
> healers."

**Context/background:** Continues the healer/tank dynamics of CASE-001 (Vanilla/
Classic-era 40-mans), but from the angle of **career progression inside the guild**
and how leaders kept the burdened role trapped there. "Roll on DPS gear" = being
allowed to compete for damage-dealer loot (the path to actually playing DPS);
"advance through the ranks" = guild rank/officer progression; "parses/metrics" =
combat-log damage rankings (e.g., WarcraftLogs-style) proving DPS competence.

## What it means, restated

Four linked mechanisms, all in service of **keeping the essential-but-burdened
worker trapped in place**:

1. **Hollow promises / perpetual deferral.** Advancement was dangled ("next season")
   to buy compliance now, with no intention of delivering — because the person is
   more valuable *stuck*.
2. **Talent-hoarding despite proven competence.** Even with metrics showing the
   healer was an equally good DPS, leaders refused to release them from healing —
   the org's convenience overrode the individual's demonstrated case for advancement.
3. **"Performance improvement plan" as a control/retention tool.** A process framed
   as *development* was used to **keep people stuck** in the detrimental role rather
   than move them up.
4. **Scapegoating the burdened role.** Systemic failures (wipes) were reflexively
   blamed on healers ("learn to heal," "lazy healers") — deflecting from strategy,
   comp, or leadership.
