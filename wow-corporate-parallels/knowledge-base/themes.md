# Cross-cutting Themes

Patterns that surface across multiple cases. As cases accumulate, the same
underlying forces should recur — those recurring forces become the backbone of
the article. Add a theme here when it shows up in 2+ cases; link the cases.

## Themes

### THEME: Compliance-vs-incentive substitution
- **What it is:** when a role's burden outstrips its rewards, weak leadership
  treats the resulting shortage as a *compliance* problem (mandates, monitoring,
  loyalty rhetoric) rather than an *incentive* problem (reward, recognition,
  lighter load). It's cheaper short-term and costs leaders nothing they'd rather
  keep — so under pressure they reliably pick it, and it always fails.
- **Shows up in:** CASE-001.
- **WoW expression:** DKP/attendance mandates, spec lock-in, loot restrictions,
  and self-farmed consumable requirements aimed at forcing scarce healers/tanks to
  show up — instead of making those roles desirable.
- **Corporate expression:** "we're a family / go the extra mile," on-call
  mandates, and surveillance loaded onto scarce load-bearing roles instead of pay,
  recognition, and load relief.
- **Why they match:** coercion attacks the symptom (attendance/presence) while
  worsening the cause (the role is now even less rewarding); the capable, who have
  the lowest exit cost, leave first.

### THEME: Loyalty/duty rhetoric as extraction
- **What it is:** "family, loyalty, duty, the extra mile" reframes an extractive
  ask as a moral one, so declining bad terms feels like betrayal.
- **Shows up in:** CASE-001.
- **WoW expression:** appeals to guild "family" and loyalty to keep people in
  unrewarding roles.
- **Corporate expression:** the "we are a family" red flag; research shows loyal
  employees are measurably targeted for more unpaid work.
- **Why they match:** emotional framing converts a rational refusal into a
  perceived moral failing, suppressing exit and complaint.

### THEME: The managerial-caste double standard
- **What it is:** the burden-bearing caste is scrutinized and controlled; the
  privileged caste exempts itself and captures the scarce rewards.
- **Shows up in:** CASE-001.
- **WoW expression:** pre-raid gear inspections and flask checks for healers/tanks
  but not DPS; leaders playing the comfortable role while forcing others into the
  hard one; loot reserved for leaders and "trustees."
- **Corporate expression:** monitoring the ICs while leadership is unaccountable;
  bonuses/promotions concentrating at the top; "do as I say" hypocrisy.
- **Why they match:** visible double standards destroy perceived fairness, after
  which the best performers disengage and people self-protect instead of perform.

### THEME: Systemic fix, not willpower (the collapse only ends when incentives change)
- **What it is:** coercion-driven dysfunction persists until the underlying
  reward *system* changes — leaders "trying harder" never resolves it.
- **Shows up in:** CASE-001.
- **WoW expression:** the healer/tank crisis eased only when Blizzard re-itemized
  and rebalanced hybrids/tanks (a system patch), not when guild leaders reformed.
- **Corporate expression:** retention collapse reverses only with real
  compensation/structure change, not pep talks or values posters.
- **Why they match:** the behavior is a rational response to incentives; change
  the incentives or the behavior stands.

## Candidate theme buckets (to organize into as we go)

These are likely clustering points based on the project's thesis — not claims yet,
just anticipated bins to sort cases into:

- **Recruitment & selection** — who you let in, trials, cultural fit vs. skill.
- **Retention & attrition** — why people leave, burnout, guild/company loyalty.
- **Leadership & authority** — GM/officer roles vs. management hierarchy; earned
  vs. positional authority.
- **Reward systems** — loot councils / DKP vs. compensation, promotions, recognition.
- **Accountability & performance** — parses/logs vs. metrics and reviews; benching.
- **Communication under pressure** — raid-night comms vs. incident/crisis management.
- **Politics & factions** — officer cliques, drama, guild splits vs. office politics
  and reorgs.
- **Culture & norms** — unwritten rules, standards, "the way we raid here."
- **Volunteer vs. paid motivation** — the key disanalogy to keep testing.
