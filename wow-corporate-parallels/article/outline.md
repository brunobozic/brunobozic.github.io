## Working title (candidates)

- **"The Guild Is the Org"**
- "Raiding Taught Me How Companies Actually Work"
- "What Fifteen Years of Herding Raiders Taught Me About Bad Management"
- "The Healer Always Quits First" *(current pick for the draft)*

## Core thesis

A World of Warcraft raiding guild is a voluntary organization solving the same
human-coordination problems a company solves — recruitment, reward, retention,
accountability, dissent — but compressed, transparent (combat logs), and with the
exits wide open. Because of that, it surfaces organizational pathologies *faster and
more clearly* than the workplace. The specific pathology the piece traces: how an
essential, under-rewarded role (the healer) gets **coerced in, gatekept, trapped,
sealed off from exit, and silenced** — and why the *seat*, not the person, produces
the leaders who do it.

## Structure

1. **Hook** — a raid-night collapse: the healers log off onto DPS alts, and 39 people
   go home. A scene any manager who's lost their one indispensable engineer will recognize.
2. **The claim** — a guild is a company with the serial numbers filed off: same
   problems, faster clock, open exits.
3. **Why the guild is the cleaner lab** — voluntary participation + fast feedback +
   transparent performance data. *(Framing research: HBR "Leadership's Online Labs";
   Reeves/Malone/O'Driscoll; Castronova's virtual-worlds-as-labs; Seely Brown & Thomas.)*
4. **The enclosure** — how the essential role gets treated, in five moves:
   - **Coerced in** (CASE-001): scarce healers met with DKP/attendance mandates,
     spec+loot lock-in, self-farmed consumables, "family/duty" rhetoric — *incentive
     problem treated as a compliance problem.* (Loyalty→exploitation; nurse
     mandatory-overtime→exit; Blizzard "Call to Arms" as the incentives counter-move.)
   - **Gatekept as a lower caste** (CASE-002): GearScore/RaiderIO bars inflated above
     what the content drops — a catch-22 that serves the gatekeeper's risk. (Credential
     inflation; signaling; the 37% connected-CEO stat; the engineer-gauntlet-vs-exec-dinner.)
   - **Trapped in role** (CASE-004): hollow "next season" promises, hoarded because
     "can't afford to lose a healer," a punitive "performance improvement plan,"
     scapegoated for wipes. (Psychological-contract breach; talent-hoarding +123%;
     PIP-as-managed-out; blame culture.)
   - **Can't leave** (CASE-005): leavers vilified, peers deputized to police, proxies
     absorb the rage, reputations destroyed. (Concertive control; moral disengagement /
     dirty work; work-devotion norm; retaliation + noncompetes.)
   - **Can't speak up** (CASE-006): the healer names the exploitation accurately and is
     punished *for naming it*, recoded as "immature/selfish." (Challenging voice →
     lower ratings (Burris); shoot-the-messenger; do-gooder derogation.)
5. **The twist / the engine** (CASE-003) — "you'd assume these were unusually bad
   people. They weren't." Why immature guild leaders and C-suite execs reason alike:
   the *seat* (unaccountable authority + public identity investment) produces the
   cognition. (Escalation of commitment; power degrades feedback (Keltner); hubris
   worse under weak oversight; narcissism predicts *emergence* not *effectiveness*.)
6. **Where the analogy breaks** (the honest section — pulls disanalogies from every
   case): pay exists at work (makes corporate coercion *more* culpable, not less);
   raiding is opt-in and low-stakes (you can log off; you can't quit rent); exit costs
   differ by orders of magnitude; games' demographics aren't representative; in-game
   "leadership" lacks legal/HR/financial constraints. Keep the framing critiques here.
7. **So what** — for leaders: the tells (compliance scored as competence; the messenger
   punished; the double standard; deniable peer enforcement) and the counter-model
   (psychological safety is structural, not a poster — Edmondson; fix incentives, not
   compliance). For the burdened: what the healer's fast exit teaches about the slow one.
8. **Close** — back to the raid-night scene: the healers were right, and the guild that
   couldn't hear them died on schedule — the same schedule companies die on, only slower.

## Tone & rules

- General reader; gloss every WoW term once (guild, raid, healer, DPS, tank, DKP,
  parse/logs, GearScore, reroll, gquit).
- Honest, analytical, first-person where the observation is the author's; never dress
  first-hand testimony as proven fact.
- Weave sources readably (name the finding, not a citation dump); keep the boundary
  conditions visible so the piece reads earned, not preachy.
- Target ~3,500–5,000 words.
